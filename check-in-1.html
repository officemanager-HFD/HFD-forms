How our check-ins work

Check-ins are a two-way conversation. They exist to improve how we work together, give you feedback you can act on, and make sure expectations are clear on both sides — no surprises. They are not a compensation discussion. Pay increases are tied to core value alignment and results, and are handled separately.

The rhythm: 15–30 minutes, every 4–6 weeks. The team member schedules the check-in from the leadership schedule. If you'll be off on the day of your scheduled check-in, it's on you to switch with another team member and let leadership know so the calendar gets updated.

What we ask of the team member

  1. Schedule your check-ins from the leadership schedule your leader provides.
  2. Be on time.
  3. Fill out this check-in agenda and get it to your leader one day before you meet.
  4. Come ready to run the meeting from your agenda.

What we ask of the leader

  1. Provide the check-in schedule and keep it updated.
  2. Be on time.
  3. Let the team member lead the conversation.
  4. Offer support and give feedback when it's needed.

Right People Scorecard

Our core purpose: At Hudsonville Family Dentistry, we provide high-quality dental care in a comfortable and caring environment while building trusted relationships and helping our patients maintain healthy smiles that support their overall health for a lifetime.

Completed by your leader and reviewed with you at the check-in. This is a coaching tool, not a criticism tool. It looks at two separate questions: does this person live our core values (right person), and are they getting results in this role (right seat)? Score the behavior against the value or the role — never the person. Schedule the next check-in before you review this with them.

Any score of 2 or 0 needs an explanation or an example. A 1 doesn't. That's what makes the conversation useful — the score alone doesn't tell us anything we can act on.

2 — Above expectations: exhibits this most if not all of the time.   1 — Meets expectations: exhibits this often, though sometimes not.   0 — Below expectations: rarely exhibits this.

Right seat — gets results (GWC)

A person thrives in a role only when all three are true. Getting it and wanting it can't be taught — capacity sometimes can, if we're willing to invest the time. Same rule here: a 2 or a 0 needs an explanation or an example.

Gets it

They understand the role — the ins and outs, why it matters, and how it fits with everyone else. Not everyone gets every role, and that isn't a character problem.

Explanation or example (required for a 2 or a 0)
Wants it

They genuinely want to do this job. You can't pay, motivate, or talk someone into wanting it — sometimes a person gets it and has the capacity but no longer wants the role.

Explanation or example (required for a 2 or a 0)
Capacity to do it

They have the time, knowledge, skill, and emotional bandwidth the role demands. Capacity gaps can occasionally be closed with training and time, if we commit to it.
